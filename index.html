Experiment 2 of 4 · The polish test

Does correcting a real student's writing make it look like a machine's?

Asked
If only the spelling, grammar and flow are corrected, does the verdict move?
Built
Claude fixes grammar (0.6% of characters), then also flow (5%). The 2025 detector judges.
Data
20 provably human passages, all pre-ChatGPT: 10 school essays, 10 PhD thesis pages.
Found
Flagged as machine: 2 → 3 → 6 of 20. Four writers flip to near-certain machine (p < 0.001).

Experiment 4 of 4 · The mirror audit

Who gets accused, and by which generation of detector?

Asked
Across detector families, which real writing is called machine-made?
Built
Three families scored under rules fixed before scoring: two 2023-era predictability methods and the 2025 classifier.
Data
52 real human passages, 52 matched Claude passages, 4 exhibits: the US Constitution, the Declaration, the first author's 2018 MPhil thesis.
Found
The older family flags the Constitution and misses 9 in 10 Claude passages. The 2025 detector is fair today: it trained on this year's models.
View
Hover a cell for the count, the rate and its 95% interval.
RegisteredHypothesis H2, that the 2025 classifier flags school essays above its 5% line, was not confirmed: 1 of 16. The older families' results are reported as registered secondary readings.

The verdict

The Turing test, both ways

JudgeAskedResult
A human judgeCan you tell machine writing from a student's?Missed 94 percent
A machine judgeCan you tell?Reads style, accuses the corrected human, misses the persona, blind to the next generation
The vendor's keyWhose thinking is this?Answers “processed by Claude”, never “thought by a student”
When neither judge can decide, the origin of a text is undecidable from the text. That is the Textual Singularity.
It is a validity failure, not a policing failure. “Is this AI?” was the wrong question.
Writing can no longer prove who did the thinking.
So assess the thinker, not the text.

Six requirements, one for each standard objection

Fluency bias
Score content, never fluency
A rubric that marks what was understood, and a bias audit before any spoken deployment.
Gaming and coaching
Counter-question, never a fixed list
Polish cannot answer a follow-up. Resistance to coaching is a quantity to measure.
The black box
Every mark quotes the words that earned it
Time-stamped evidence for each mark, in the student's own words.
Reliability
Conduct first, judge afterwards
No score during the session; grading is one pass over the full transcript, re-runnable.
Oversight
The teacher signs off; the student can contest
The record belongs to the student: video, transcript, rubric, marks, feedback.
Privacy
Consented, scoped, deletable
Retention with a real deletion mechanism.

Ask the thinker · Why now

The oral examination never scaled. The reason has just been removed.

1984
Bloom measures what one-to-one tutoring buys a learner, and names the cost that keeps it rare: an expert's time, per student.

The viva has the same cost. It has always been the assessment we could not afford.
Speech to text
The student is heard, verbatim, time-stamped.
Text to speech
The examiner speaks, in real time.
An avatar
The examiner has a face; the student answers a person.
A model that follows up
The examiner can counter-question, not just ask a list.
What was scarce was examiner time. It is not scarce any more. The conditions of production can now be secured at the scale of everyday coursework.
The open question: does an AI examiner judge as validly as expert human examiners in a live viva?
The examining instrument and its validation are the subject of a university-led research programme, currently under review for funding.
Writing still builds thinking; we keep it. But when text stops proving thought, assessment should ask the thinker.
